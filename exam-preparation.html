Exam Preparation: Your 4-Week Action Plan

📅 April 15, 2026 ⏱ 10 min read ✍️ Elite STEM Tutors

Four weeks out from exam day is not a crisis — it's a golden window. Students who use this period strategically don't just pass; they dominate. This guide gives you a week-by-week blueprint to walk into your exam confident, prepared, and in control.

1 Week

Audit & Map the Battlefield

Days 1–7 · Foundation & Diagnosis

Before you can study smarter, you need to know exactly what you're up against. Week one is about ruthless honesty — identifying your strongest and weakest areas so that every hour of study counts.

  • Collect your syllabus, past papers, and mark schemes for every topic on the exam
  • Do a timed diagnostic test under real exam conditions to expose genuine weak spots
  • Colour-code topics: Green (confident), Amber (shaky), Red (need to rebuild from scratch)
  • Build a four-week study timetable — assign more sessions to Red topics, maintenance sessions to Green ones
  • Gather all resources: textbooks, notes, online videos, and your tutor's materials in one organised space
  • Set a non-negotiable daily study block and protect it fiercely
2 Week

Deep Work on Weak Zones

Days 8–14 · Concept Reconstruction

This is where most students go wrong — they skim over hard topics hoping they won't appear. Week two demands the opposite. Attack your Red topics head-on while the exam is still far enough away to make a real difference.

  • Begin every session with your weakest topic — your focus is sharpest at the start
  • Use the Feynman Technique: explain each concept out loud as if teaching a 12-year-old
  • Create concept maps linking formulas, definitions, and worked examples for STEM topics
  • Do at least five targeted practice questions per topic before moving on
  • Schedule a mid-week check-in with your tutor to get expert feedback on specific gaps
  • End each day with a 10-minute review of what you covered — no screens, just recall
💡 Elite Insight

Research in cognitive science consistently shows that spaced repetition outperforms marathon cramming by a factor of 2–4x in long-term retention. Spreading your study across four weeks isn't just less stressful — it's neurologically superior to last-minute revision.

3 Week

Past Papers & Active Recall

Days 15–21 · Exam Simulation

By week three, your knowledge base is rebuilding. Now it's time to stress-test it. The single highest-return activity a student can do in the lead-up to an exam is timed past paper practice — and that starts now.

  • Complete a full past paper every two days under strict timed, closed-book conditions
  • Mark each paper using the official mark scheme — be brutal and honest with yourself
  • Log every mark you drop in an "error journal" and categorise it: silly mistake, knowledge gap, or misread question
  • Revisit the mark schemes to learn the exact phrasing examiners reward
  • Replace passive re-reading with active recall — close the book and write down everything you know from memory
  • Use flashcards (physical or Anki) for definitions, formulas, and key equations in STEM subjects
4 Week

Polish, Rest & Game-Day Prep

Days 22–28 · Final Consolidation

The worst thing you can do in the final week is panic and start learning new content. Week four is about sharpening what you already know, managing your mental state, and walking into the exam room feeling like you own it.

  • Review your error journal — these are your highest-probability exam risks, so fix them now
  • Do one final full past paper two days before the exam, then stop and let your brain consolidate
  • Create a one-page "cheat sheet" of formulas and key facts per subject (the act of making it is the revision)
  • Prioritise 8 hours of sleep every night — sleep is when memory consolidation actually happens
  • Prepare everything the night before: stationery, ID, water, a light meal plan for exam day
  • On the morning of the exam, review your one-pager, do a 10-minute walk, and arrive early

Habits That Work Across All Four Weeks

Beyond the week-by-week structure, these non-negotiable habits will compound your results throughout the entire preparation window.

🧠

Pomodoro Sessions

Study in 45-minute focused blocks with 10-minute breaks. Prevents burnout and maintains deep focus.

📵

Phone in Another Room

Merely having your phone visible reduces cognitive capacity. Remove it — not silence it.

💧

Hydration & Nutrition

Even mild dehydration impairs concentration. Drink water consistently throughout every study session.

🏃

Daily Movement

30 minutes of physical activity increases BDNF — the brain's learning protein. Walk, run, anything.

📖

Teach to Learn

Explaining a concept to a peer or even a wall exposes gaps faster than re-reading ever will.

🎯

Set Session Goals

Start every study session with a specific outcome, not a time target. "Master quadratic equations" beats "study for 2 hours."

A Final Word on Mindset

Exam preparation is as much a psychological challenge as an academic one. Students who perform at their highest level share one trait: they treat exam preparation as a skill, not a personality trait. You are not "bad at exams." You may simply never have had a structured system — until now.

Four weeks is enough time to shift from anxious to in command. Start with week one today. Be honest about your gaps. Put in the work. And trust the process.
